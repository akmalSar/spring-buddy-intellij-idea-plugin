Reports common AssertJ anti-patterns and rewrites them to the idiomatic matcher.

Examples:

  • assertThat(x.equals(y)).isTrue() → assertThat(x).isEqualTo(y)
  • assertThat(list.size()).isEqualTo(0) → assertThat(list).isEmpty()
  • assertThat(list.size()).isEqualTo(3) → assertThat(list).hasSize(3)
  • assertThat(opt.isPresent()).isTrue() → assertThat(opt).isPresent()
  • assertThat(str).isEqualTo("") → assertThat(str).isEmpty()
  • assertThat(str.isEmpty()).isTrue() → assertThat(str).isEmpty()
  • assertThat(coll.contains(x)).isTrue() → assertThat(coll).contains(x)

The richer matcher gives a much better failure message than a plain boolean assertion.
